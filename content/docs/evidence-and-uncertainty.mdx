Epidemiologists use this page to read a signal, separate missing evidence from a low observed value, and name what the release leaves unknown.

One Health Atlas helps you find places where available tick, vector, and related One Health evidence may warrant review. A signal starts an investigation question.

<Callout title="Interpret with care" type="warn">
  Atlas does not provide clinical advice, person-level exposure or disease-risk
  predictions, causal proof, guaranteed outbreak forecasts, or an automatic
  public-health decision.
</Callout>

## What an Atlas output means

| Term | Meaning | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Observed signal** | A value or status from an approved source, such as a published county-linked human-surveillance value, tick status, or pathogen detection status. | Name what the source reported, with its geography and time or vintage. |
| **Derived measure** | A value calculated from released inputs, such as a county review score or evidence-completeness percentage. | Use it to focus review. It is not a diagnosis, a complete disease-burden estimate, a confidence interval, or individual risk. |
| **Contextual evidence** | Background that helps you read a result, such as population, access, rurality, habitat, climate, or scientific context. | Keep its own source, coverage, geography, vintage, and limitations. |
| **Modeled output** | An estimate, forecast, classification, or other model result, rather than a directly published observation. | Require the model version, inputs, validation, uncertainty, and human review. The current public explorer does not promise an authoritative modeled outbreak or individual-risk output. Signed-in Atlas Explorer can show a published model-assisted surveillance review priority when its release matches the selected evidence release. Atlas withholds a tier from a different release, and withholds it when the governed release for the page is not known. That tier is review prioritization, not disease risk or predicted incidence. |
| **Investigation candidate** | A geography or pattern selected because the available evidence, or an evidence gap, merits a bounded follow-up question. | State what to check next and what evidence could change the interpretation. |

[Atlas Explorer](/explore) labels the county review score and evidence completeness as analytical signals. The active release is shown in the application. [Current capabilities](/docs/current-capabilities) describes the release boundary.

## Start with provenance

Record these facts before you interpret a signal:

- **Source and publisher:** who produced or published the input, and the link or citation.
- **Release and vintage:** the active Atlas release and the source observation, reporting, or publication period.
- **Geography:** county, state, sampling location, boundary, and records not allocated to a county.
- **Method and version:** the methodology, calculation, or model version behind the displayed value.
- **Freshness and processing context:** source retrieval, release generation, and data load times when the application provides them.
- **Limitations:** coverage, missingness, suppression, comparability, uncertainty, and known data-quality constraints.

The evidence snapshot summarizes source periods, scoring methodology, and processing times. Exact IDs sit behind **Inspect provenance**. A generated date is the release, not necessarily the observation date. See [Releases and methodology labels](/docs/releases-and-methodology).

A later governed source can use the same concepts and still keep its publisher, vintage, geography, period, transformation, and limitations. A shared model does not make two sources equivalent. Missing, suppressed, unavailable, and uncertain inputs stay visible as the states below. See [sources and canonical evidence](/docs/sources-and-canonical-evidence).

## Read value states carefully

These states answer different questions. Keep them separate.

| State | What it can mean | What you should say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Observed or published** | A source published a value or status for the stated geography and period. | “This source reports…” and include the source context. |
| **Zero** | The source explicitly reported zero for the stated measure and period. | “The published value is zero for this release.” Coverage and method still bound that zero. |
| **Missing or no record** | No county-linked row or value is available in the release. | “No county record is available.” Do not call this absence. |
| **Suppressed or privacy-protected** | A publisher withheld or transformed a value to protect privacy or meet a reporting rule. | Name the protection. Do not reconstruct a precise count. |
| **Unavailable or not reported** | The source, field, geography, or period is not in this release. | Treat it as an evidence gap, not as zero or no activity. |
| **Stale** | The vintage or retrieval is too old for the question. | Name the time limitation and confirm the current source. |
| **Sparse or incomplete** | Coverage, sampling, reporting, or scored inputs are limited. | State what the records can and cannot support. |
| **Disagreement** | Two sources or signals point in different directions. | Keep both observations and make the disagreement the question. |

Atlas Explorer uses “No records,” “Unavailable,” and completeness indicators in context. “No records” describes published evidence availability. It does not establish that ticks, pathogens, or disease are absent.

After a successful response, the workspace uses **Available** (including a published zero), **Limited** (a signal plus a documented constraint), and **Unavailable** (absent or out of scope, shown as that word rather than zero). Loading and error are not the Unavailable label. See [the professional workspace](/docs/professional-workspace).

## Low signal is not the same as insufficient surveillance

A low observed signal means a source reported a low value or status under its method and coverage. **Insufficient surveillance** means the evidence is too incomplete, unrepresentative, outdated, or incomparable to tell whether the underlying activity is low.

A county with no county-linked published Lyme case record may have zero cases, suppressed records, unallocated records, a reporting-boundary mismatch, or another publication limitation. Atlas does not treat that missing record as zero by default. A tick table with no county record does not prove ticks are absent.

When evidence is incomplete, use the safer statement: “The current release does not establish this.” Then name the source or coverage check that would tell you more.

## Why signals may not agree

Inputs can differ in design, boundaries, periods, definitions, coverage, and suppression. That does not prove one source caused another. Compare provenance first. Do not average away a disagreement.

## Read the evidence before acting

Name the question and the output. Check provenance, value state, and comparability. State what remains unknown. Choose source validation, local review, a time-bounded analysis, expert consultation, or no action. Record the handoff with the [investigation workflows](/docs/investigation-workflows).

[Atlas Explorer](/explore) shows the map and the county list for the same release. If the map is unavailable, use the list. For an AI-enabled experience, keep the [AI Ethics commitments](/ai-ethics) in view.
