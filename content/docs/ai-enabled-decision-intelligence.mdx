title: AI-enabled decision intelligence
description: Understand what AI means in One Health Atlas, what is available now, and which future capabilities still require release and human review.

One Health Atlas is an **Epidemiologist Decision Intelligence Platform**. The product direction is AI-enabled: it is intended to help people organize, compare, synthesize, and explain governed One Health evidence so they can decide what deserves investigation.

AI-enabled does not mean that Atlas replaces epidemiologists or turns a score into a prediction. The preferred public-health framing is:

**signals → evidence → hypotheses → anomaly candidates → investigation priorities**

Each step must remain interpretable, bounded by the evidence, clear about uncertainty, and accountable to human review.

## Capability status

| Capability | Status | What you can rely on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transparent follow-up-priority scoring | Available now | The current score is a deterministic, adjustable calculation over released inputs. It helps focus review; it is not a predictive model, diagnosis, causal claim, disease-burden estimate, or individual-risk output. |
| Model-assisted surveillance review priority | Available now | Signed-in Atlas Explorer can show a persisted HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW tier for the selected county and on county evidence when that tier's release matches the selected evidence release. Atlas withholds the tier when the returned release differs from the evidence on screen, and when the governed release for the page is not known. A withheld, missing, or not-estimable result is not shown as LOW. The tier is review prioritization, not disease risk, predicted incidence, a diagnosis, or an autonomous decision. It is separate from the rules-based county review score. |
| Public evidence and provenance context | Available now | The Explorer provides released signals, source/release/method context, limitations, and a table path for reviewing results. Start with [evidence, provenance, and uncertainty](/docs/evidence-and-uncertainty). |
| Atlas Assistant: reviewed literature | Early access, feature-gated | `/assistant` can ask the Atlas API about reviewed PubMed and PMC Open Access literature when enabled. Answers include cited evidence and explicit unavailable-evidence outcomes. This does not query live Atlas geography or scores, provide medical advice, or replace human review. |
| Ask Atlas in the professional workspace | Experimental/demo | Atlas Explorer and Compare open the dedicated [Ask Atlas page](/askatlas). When literature chat is enabled, it uses that same reviewed-literature service and leaves navigation with you. When it is disabled, the Ask Atlas page says Ask Atlas is unavailable and the other workspace pages stay usable. The request is a message plus optional conversation history. There is no Structured mode and no Both mode. |

The status labels are intentionally explicit. A planned architecture, a design exploration, or a local demo is not evidence that a capability is available in the public release.

## What the current release does

Today, Atlas helps you:

- explore public county and state patterns;
- review human disease, tick/vector, pathogen, environmental, population, and related context when released;
- inspect the source, vintage, geography, release, method, completeness, and limitations behind a result;
- compare supported geographies descriptively; and
- turn a review-priority signal or evidence gap into a bounded investigation question.

See [current capabilities](/docs/current-capabilities) and [investigation workflows](/docs/investigation-workflows) for the released behavior and task steps.

## What human review means

Use an AI-assisted or analytical output as a prompt for review. A responsible reviewer should:

1. check the source and provenance;
2. verify that the geography, period, coverage, and method fit the question;
3. distinguish observed evidence from a derived score, contextual evidence, or modeled output;
4. state missing evidence and uncertainty rather than filling gaps with assumptions; and
5. decide whether source validation, local surveillance review, additional analysis, expert consultation, or no action is appropriate.

If an output cannot show the evidence and limits needed for that review, treat it as unavailable. Do not infer certainty from fluent language, a rank, a map color, or an AI-generated summary.

## What Atlas AI is not for

Atlas does not:

- diagnose Lyme disease or provide clinical or treatment advice;
- predict an individual's exposure, infection, or disease risk;
- guarantee that an outbreak will or will not occur;
- prove that a vector, pathogen, environment, or population factor caused an outcome;
- convert missing surveillance into evidence of absence or zero; or
- make an automatic public-health decision or replace epidemiologists, analysts, local knowledge, or responsible authorities.

The [AI Ethics commitments](/ai-ethics) are the public page for AI status, boundaries, and open decisions. The professional workspace footer opens that same page. Privacy and data-use commitments belong on the [Privacy page](/privacy); this guide does not add to either policy source.
