---
title: The Atlas decision-support workflow
description: Follow Atlas Lyme from fragmented One Health evidence to a focused epidemiological investigation question.
---

Atlas is organized around a decision-support path:

`DATA → SIGNALS → CONTEXT → INTERPRETATION → INVESTIGATION → PUBLIC-HEALTH ACTION`

Each step answers a different question. Moving through the steps helps prevent a visually prominent value from being mistaken for a complete conclusion.

## 1. Data — what is available?

Atlas brings together public evidence that may include human disease, ticks and vectors, pathogens, climate, habitat, population, and scientific literature. Start by checking what the current release actually contains: source, geography, time period, update context, and limitations.

Data availability is not the same thing as disease absence. A missing, suppressed, stale, or incomparable value must remain visibly different from a measured zero.

## 2. Signals — what stands out?

The application can surface geographic patterns, ranked counties, review-priority scores, and other analytical signals. A signal is a way to focus attention across many places or measures. It is not a final epidemiological conclusion.

When a signal is based on multiple inputs, review the inputs and the methodology version that produced it. Do not infer that a higher score is a probability, incidence rate, individual risk, or disease-burden estimate unless the evidence and method explicitly say so.

## 3. Context — what else should be considered?

Use the available One Health context to understand what may be relevant to the signal: vector or pathogen evidence, environmental conditions, population context, and related scientific evidence. Context can make a question more specific, but association or co-occurrence alone does not establish causation.

## 4. Interpretation — what does the evidence support?

Before writing an interpretation, check:

- Does the source cover the same geography and time period?
- Is the release current enough for this question?
- Are the measures comparable in definition, denominator, and method?
- Are any values missing, suppressed, sparse, stale, or unavailable?
- What does the methodology permit you to say, and what does it not permit?

The safest interpretation is often conditional: “This area is worth reviewing because these available signals align,” followed by the evidence and limitations that a reviewer should verify.

## 5. Investigation — what should happen next?

Turn the interpretation into a bounded question. For example:

- Which surveillance or laboratory records should be reviewed next?
- Is additional vector or pathogen sampling warranted?
- Does local environmental or population context change the priority for follow-up?
- Which source or time period would reduce the most uncertainty?

Record the selected geography, release or vintage, methodology, evidence consulted, and unresolved limitations so another reviewer can reproduce the starting point.

## 6. Public-health action — what is appropriate?

Atlas can help a team decide what deserves investigation. It does not authorize an intervention or make a public-health decision automatically. Action belongs to the responsible epidemiologists, agencies, and partners who can incorporate local knowledge, additional data, operational constraints, and applicable policy.

## A repeatable review pattern

For a defensible review, use this short loop:

1. **Select** a supported geography or question in the [Atlas Explorer](/).
2. **Scan** the signal and the equivalent table or text summary.
3. **Inspect** evidence, provenance, methodology, and limitations.
4. **Compare** only measures that are sufficiently aligned in definition, geography, and time.
5. **State uncertainty** and distinguish missing evidence from a measured zero.
6. **Write** the investigation question and the next evidence needed.
7. **Share** the supported URL or export with the review context intact.

For the detailed boundaries behind this workflow, see [What is Atlas Lyme?](/docs/what-is-atlas) and [Who is Atlas for?](/docs/who-is-atlas-for).
