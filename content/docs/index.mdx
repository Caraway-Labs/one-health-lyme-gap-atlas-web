Atlas Lyme is an **Epidemiologist Decision Intelligence Platform** for Lyme disease and other tick-borne diseases. It brings fragmented human disease, tick/vector, pathogen, climate, habitat, population, and scientific evidence together so epidemiologists can understand what is happening, investigate why, and decide what deserves closer review.

Our product vision is **from fragmented surveillance data to timely, interpretable, evidence-backed public-health action**. Atlas is being built to identify what matters, explain why it matters, communicate uncertainty, and help epidemiologists decide what deserves investigation.

The platform supports a practical path from `data` to `signals`, `context`, `interpretation`, `investigation`, and—when the evidence supports it—public-health action.

## Who Atlas is for

Atlas is designed for epidemiologists, public-health surveillance teams, researchers, and other partners who need to review geographic signals without losing sight of the evidence quality behind them.

Use Atlas when you need to ask:

- What is happening, and where?
- What evidence supports that interpretation?
- How current, complete, and comparable is the evidence?
- Which areas or signals may warrant investigation next?

## What Atlas is — and is not

Atlas is a decision-support platform, not an autonomous decision-maker. It helps users organize and interpret public evidence; it does not replace epidemiologists, public-health analysts, or domain experts.

Atlas helps users explore patterns in public evidence at the county level. A review-priority score or other analytical signal is a structured way to focus questions; it is not a diagnosis, an individual exposure or disease-risk prediction, a measure of disease burden, or proof of causation.

The current release is an early public prototype and situational-awareness foundation. Capability pages distinguish verified public behavior from planned, in-development, and experimental work. Atlas should help identify signals and investigation candidates—not claim that an outbreak will occur or that a geography is definitively safe or unsafe.

## A quick start

Public Explorer:

1. Open the [Atlas Explorer](/overview) and choose a state or county.
2. Review the county list, map, and evidence panels together. The table and text summaries provide the same key findings without requiring a map.
3. Inspect the evidence behind a signal, including its source, release or time period, methodology, and limitations.
4. Share the URL when you want another person to review the same supported geography and filter state.

Signed-in workspace:

1. Open [Review](/app/review) and choose the United States or one state.
2. Use the map and the county list together, then open Investigate for one county.
3. Use Explore to scan a measure, or Compare for two counties. Read [the professional workspace](/docs/professional-workspace) for which routes are released.
4. Open **Docs** from the workspace navigation when you need this reference. It opens in a new tab and leaves the review address in the original tab.

## Read the evidence carefully

Atlas combines measures that can have different geographies, years, publishers, and update schedules. The application displays the release and methodology information available for the current result. Check the source and vintage before comparing measures.

### Missing is not zero

An absent published county record can reflect zero reported cases, suppression, an unavailable source, a different reporting boundary, or another limitation. Do not treat a missing value as proof that no disease or surveillance activity exists.

### Start with provenance

For a defensible follow-up question, record the source, source vintage, geography, retrieval or load context when available, methodology version, and known limitations. Use the in-application data dictionary and methodology sections for context that must stay close to the result.

## Developer and agent access

The public REST API is documented by its owning service. Start with [API, MCP, and access](/docs/api-mcp-and-access) and the machine-readable [OpenAPI schema](https://api.carawaylabs.com/openapi.json). Public reads do not use an API key. The Atlas MCP repository publishes a development server with smoke tools only; REST remains the supported interface for Atlas data. Settings does not generate API keys in this release.
