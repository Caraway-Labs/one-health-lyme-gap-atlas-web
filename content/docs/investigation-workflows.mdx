title: Investigation workflows
description: Use One Health Atlas to move from a surveillance question to a bounded, evidence-backed investigation next step.
---

Epidemiologists use this page to turn a broad surveillance question into a reviewable next step in the signed-in workspace.

[Atlas Explorer](/explore) holds the operating picture, county evidence, and suggested follow-up. [Compare](/compare) holds two counties. The [professional workspace](/docs/professional-workspace) guide lists the six destinations. For status, see [current capabilities](/docs/current-capabilities). For the product model, see [the decision-support workflow](/docs/atlas-workflow).

<Callout title="Interpret with care" type="warn">
  A rank or score focuses attention. It does not mean the county has the highest
  disease burden, that missing evidence means zero, or that a cause has been
  identified.
</Callout>

<Steps>
<Step>

## 1. Find a geography or signal for review

**Question:** Where should the team look first in this release?

1. Open [Atlas Explorer](/explore) and choose the United States, a state, or a county.
2. Read the map and the county list. Open county evidence. Suggested follow-up is on that page.
3. If the map is unavailable, use the county list. It is the non-map path.
4. Record the release, methodology version, geography, and the evidence that made the signal worth reviewing.

**Next step:** Name the source and time period to validate before you request follow-up.

</Step>
<Step>

## 2. Compare a county with surrounding or peer geographies

**Question:** Does this signal also appear in another county in the same release?

1. Open [Compare](/compare) and choose two counties. The page accepts two.
2. Read each value with its source, release, method, and completeness.
3. Record why you selected each county.

**Evidence check:** The comparison is descriptive. A score difference is not causation, a statistical test, or a person-level risk difference. Reporting practice, geography, vintage, and missingness can make a difference hard to compare.

**Next step:** State what new evidence would change the interpretation.

</Step>
<Step>

## 3. Inspect evidence before deciding what to investigate next

**Question:** What supports this result, and what remains uncertain?

1. Open county evidence in [Atlas Explorer](/explore).
2. Read **source, release, and methodology context**.
3. Keep “no record available” distinct from “zero.”
4. Write the gap with the question. Example: “The county is a review candidate in release X; validate the human-disease source vintage and vector coverage before interpreting the signal.”

**Evidence check:** Measures can come from different publishers, schedules, years, and scales. A result is only as interpretable as its provenance and limitations.

**Next step:** Ask a qualified reviewer to validate local meaning, then choose more analysis, field investigation, coordination, or no action.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Record and share the review

Record the URL and date, release id, methodology version, geography, filters, score assumptions, provenance, limitations, and the next decision. The URL preserves supported analytical state. It does not reproduce a later release.

Keep the [AI Ethics commitments](/ai-ethics) nearby for an experimental experience, and use [current capabilities](/docs/current-capabilities) to separate a released behavior from a planned one. Your organization remains accountable for interpretation, validation, and action.
