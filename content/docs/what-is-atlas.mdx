Epidemiologists use this page to see what One Health Atlas can do and which conclusions it leaves to the reviewer.

One Health Atlas is an **Epidemiologist Decision Intelligence Platform** for Lyme disease and other tick-borne diseases. It brings One Health evidence into one place so you can recognize patterns, read the context, and decide what deserves investigation.

The product vision is:

> From fragmented surveillance data to timely, interpretable, evidence-backed public-health action.

The North Star is:

> A system that identifies what matters, explains why it matters, communicates uncertainty, and helps epidemiologists decide what deserves investigation.

<Callout title="Interpret with care" type="warn">
  A visible signal means “review this question with the available evidence.”
  Atlas is not a clinical diagnostic tool or a substitute for medical advice; an
  individual exposure, infection, or disease-risk predictor; a direct measure of
  disease burden merely because a place ranks highly; proof that a vector,
  pathogen, environment, or population factor caused an outcome; a guarantee
  that an outbreak will or will not occur; or a replacement for epidemiologists,
  public-health analysts, local knowledge, or domain experts.
</Callout>

## Why Atlas exists

Human disease, ticks, pathogens, climate, habitat, population, and literature often come from different publishers, on different schedules, at different scales. Atlas joins those perspectives and keeps the context you need to interpret them.

## What Atlas helps you do

Depending on the release, you can review geographic patterns, bring human, ecological, environmental, pathogen, and population context into one investigation, inspect source, time, methodology, and limitations, compare supported geographies, and turn a broad question into a smaller one. You remain responsible for interpretation, follow-up, and action.

## What “AI-enabled” means here

AI-enabled is the product direction: find patterns, connect evidence, summarize context, and ask a better follow-up question. An AI-assisted experience must stay grounded in approved evidence, show its sources and limitations, and keep a human review step. The public application does not treat an AI output as an authoritative finding. A generated explanation is a prompt for review, not a diagnosis, causal conclusion, or automatic public-health decision.

The current release is an early public prototype and situational-awareness foundation.

## A public, evidence-first platform

You can explore the public experience without an account. A signed-in profile can store a default jurisdiction for the next Review session. That preference does not change evidence, scores, or which geographies you can open. Public results stay readable on the map and in an equivalent table or text path, with provenance and limitations kept close to the result.
