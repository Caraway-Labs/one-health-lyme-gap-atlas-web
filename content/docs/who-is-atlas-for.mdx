Epidemiologists and public-health analysts use this page to see which questions Atlas can help them frame, and which decisions stay with their organization.

## Primary users

Atlas is primarily for:

- epidemiologists and disease-surveillance teams;
- public-health analysts who need geographic and One Health context;
- researchers investigating relationships among disease, vectors, pathogens, environment, and populations; and
- public-health partners who need a common, reviewable starting point for discussion.

The product is built around expert review. It does not assume that every user has the same data access, local knowledge, surveillance mandate, or decision authority.

## Questions Atlas can help frame

1. **What is happening, and where?**
2. **What might help explain it?**
3. **How strong is the evidence?** Check source, vintage, geography, methodology, completeness, and limitations.
4. **What deserves a closer look?**

Atlas helps when several imperfect sources need to be reviewed together. It is less useful for a clinical diagnosis, a person-level risk assessment, or an answer the public data cannot support.

## A typical user session

In [Atlas Explorer](/explore), choose a state or county and read the map, list, and county detail together. Inspect evidence before you interpret a signal, and share the URL so another reviewer can reproduce the geography and filters. Coverage depends on the release. See [current capabilities](/docs/current-capabilities).

## Who makes the decision?

The reviewer and their organization validate the source, add local knowledge, decide whether follow-up is appropriate, and communicate action through the responsible authority. A rank, score, map color, or AI-assisted explanation does not transfer that responsibility to Atlas.

## Access and trust boundaries

The public Atlas experience stays usable without signing in. In the professional workspace, a saved default jurisdiction chooses the next Review start when the address has no scope. It does not unlock evidence, change a score, or limit which geographies you can open.

Pair every important result with its [evidence and provenance context](/docs/index#start-with-provenance). Read [the AI Ethics commitments](/ai-ethics) for the approved boundaries around AI-assisted experiences.
